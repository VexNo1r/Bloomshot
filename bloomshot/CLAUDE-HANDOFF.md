# BLOOMSHOT — handoff to Codex

Updated October 9, 2026 by Claude after passes 9 to 11 and the level campaign (levels 1 to 10 and their unlock). This replaces the October 4 handoff. Read all of "Start here" before touching files.

## Start here

Trevor wants a beautiful, vibrant, fast mobile game that people love enough to pay for optional extras, live on iPhone, iPad and Android and earning money. Players should feel in control and feel they are progressing. Code and art are AI-assisted; never claim a human team made them.

**The design bar.** Trevor's words: the UI and text must look "made by a team of humans, not AI", and the scenery and sprites should look like the work of "a very successful and professional indie company that makes 2D art games", and be creative. He chose the "Storybook game" direction on October 3. Every later pass must hold the rules in "Storybook rules" below, including store listing copy and the web page.

Keep purchases honest: exact contents and price shown, no fake scarcity, no misleading timers, no paid randomness, no frustration designed to sell relief, no pressure aimed at children. The app stores reject the rest anyway. The game is meant to appeal to all ages but is not listed in a kids category.

**Which copy is authoritative.** The GitHub repository, not any older zip or workspace copy:

- GitHub: https://github.com/VexNo1r/Bloomshot. Work on a branch and open every pull request against `main`. Do not stack pull requests on each other: Trevor merges from his phone, and on October 3 three stacked PRs merged into a feature branch instead of `main`. A branch built on an unmerged PR still targets `main` and says "merge #N first". Never force-push `main`.
- Layout: `bloomshot/` is the game, `native/` the Capacitor Android/iOS shell, `docs/` release and store notes, `.github/workflows/` CI, cloud builds, the Android release bundle and the GitHub Pages deploy.
- Web build: `.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes `bloomshot/` to https://vexno1r.github.io/Bloomshot/ once Pages is enabled with Source set to GitHub Actions (a setting only Trevor can change).
- `VexNo1r/harborline` is Trevor's separate lead-recovery product. The game never goes there.

## Saves (never reset)

Normal save `bloomshot.save.v1`; `?qa` uses `bloomshot.qa.v1`. Trevor plays in both. New fields are always additive and old saves load unchanged. Fields added since the last handoff: `garden.decor` (decorations built), `goals` (today's goals and progress, today only), `depths` (level campaign: `{ [levelId]: { stars, best, wave } }`, levels 1 to 10, cleaned on load), `garden.depthBest` (stars per level, for seed rewards), `powers` (powerup counts; a save without it starts with one of each), `powerReceipts` (the last 50 store transaction ids, so a purchase never counts twice) `powersMet` (the one-time powerups tip was shown) and `tutorial` (the first-time tutorial was finished or skipped; an old save with any played record loads as `true`, so only brand-new players see it). In the native app `native.js` mirrors every save to Capacitor Preferences and restores it if the OS wipes web storage.

## Storybook rules

- **Type.** Two self-hosted open-source fonts in `assets/fonts/` (SIL Open Font License): Fredoka for titles, buttons, tags and canvas text, Nunito for body text. No system-ui on canvas, no other fonts.
- **No tracked uppercase.** No small spaced-out capital "eyebrow" labels and no letter-spaced canvas text. Tags are pill `.card-tag`s in sentence case.
- **Short game labels.** "Level 4 clear!", "Wave 3 of 10", "Unlock levels 5 to 10 · $2.99". Hints are a few words and fit one line (wave hints at most about 50 characters). Prices and what a purchase contains stay exact.
- **Chunky tactile UI.** Pill buttons with a solid darker bottom edge that presses down (green primary, gold for purchases, cream secondary), cream panels with an inked edge, ribbon titles with notched tails, filled tab icons. Colors are tokens at the top of `styles.css`; each level also tints the page (`body[data-theme="depth-…"]`).
- **Scene art.** Painted in light and air with the kit in `scenery.js`: one key light per scene, shapes modelled toward it with gradients and a lit rim, soft shadows, far layers sinking into haze, a final grade and paper grain. No ink outlines on scenery (those belong to the pieces in play); small hand-placed creatures keep soft colored lines and their personality. Seeded randomness so a scene is identical every time, and busy detail kept to the edges and below the danger line so falling flowers read instantly.
- **No AI-generated raster art.** Interface graphics are CSS and inline SVG; scenes and sprites are Canvas paths. `ART-PROVENANCE.md` records this honestly; keep it accurate.

Screenshots: `docs/screenshots/storybook/` (interface), `docs/screenshots/levels/` (levels 1 to 4 and the map), `docs/screenshots/levels-deep/` (levels 5 to 10 and the unlock).

- Finale, aim and danger (`feel.js`): Feel (feel.js plus small hooks in app.js and art.js) handles the camera, game-time scale and the big moments. - **feel.js** is a UMD module with no DOM and no randomness. Feel.create({motion}) returns a small tween machine: request(kind, opts) takes 'finale', 'impact', 'release', 'dip' or 'punch', and step(realDt) updates `out` = {scale, zoom, fx, fy, vignette, busy, phase}. All timings are in Feel.timings. createHitStop() gives at most .09 s per hit and .09 s per rolling second; app.js's jolt() routes its freeze through it. createGovernor() turns frame times into game.quality (0, 1 or 2) and never steps down. predictHit() asks Engine.earliest whether a seed meets a flower within a horizon. - **The finale (app.js, feel block above processEvents).** finaleWatch() predicts the hit on the final flower: the boss on its last ring on a level's last wave, or the last open flower every fifth endless-Rush wave. It requests 'finale' (time to .22, zoom 1.16, dusk vignette), and feelEvent() turns the real bloom into the impact. startSweep() sets bud.flareAt for the gold light and its notes. The result is held (resultAt = Infinity) until the sweep ends and feel is idle, then waits 1.25 s, with a 4 s cap; a tap skips it. - **Danger and aim.** Losing a life writes game.wilts and game.heartBreak and flashes red. aimFeel()/aimLock() click the dial and announce lock-ons. - **art.js** draws the pod's slingshot pose and seed pull-back, the lock-on arrowheads, comet seeds, wilt ghosts, broken hearts and the screen-space veils (drawFeelOverlay). Glows, veils, wilts and streaks are sprites cached at 3x in feelSprites (OffscreenCanvas, then a page canvas, then plain paths in Node), so seed drawing makes no per-frame gradients or shadow blur. - **The camera** is applied once at the top of draw(), so the backdrop zooms too. coordinates() maps touches back with Feel.toBoard. - **Reduced motion** (save.settings.motion false) keeps feel neutral: the finale chord and title still play and the result comes after .4 s. - **Tests and checks.** qa/test-feel.cjs covers the module and plays the finale through app.js; scratch Playwright scripts were used for screenshots and the 4x-throttle trace.
- Super Bloom, tricks, bosses and the wave-6 retry (`rush.js`, `depths.js`): Super Bloom, trick shots, the chain badge, named bosses and the wave-6 checkpoint (rules package). **How crediting works.** rush.js tracks which seed earned each bloom. `this.striker` is the seed whose collision is being resolved. Pending chain items carry {by, from, via, at, depth}, so blooms that come due after a seed is gone still count for it, through `this.due`. Only seed-earned work charges the sun: a direct bloom 1, a chained bloom .5, a puffcap 1, a geode 2, a boss crack 1, a trick 4. A plain strike() with no seed behaves exactly as before. **Super Bloom.** It starts when the sun fills: the first fill of a level needs 28.8, later fills need 48. - The bloom moment (`art.js` flowers): Bloom package (art.js, qa/test-bloomfx.cjs). A bloom is stateless: `drawUnfurl(ctx, bud, age, options)` is a pure function of the bud, its age since `bloomAt`, and a seed hashed from `bud.id`. - Over .55 s it shows: a one-frame white silhouette; cracks from the struck side (impactAngle + PI); a squash and bubble-pop of the closed bud; casing shards drawn as one path per bud; then the outer and inner layers opening petal by petal from the petal facing `impactAngle` (order 0, 1, -1, 2, -2 ...); the heart pop at .2–.32 s; and a .05 s crossfade into `drawFlower(1)`. - All art comes from 3x sprites in a small LRU atlas (`atlasSprite`, keyed by type and radius) painted with the same `petal()` and `paintFlower` geometry. `spritesFor` warms every sprite a bloom needs on its first frame. - For regular buds (r ≤ 16), each layer's opening is baked lazily into a sheet of frames (`sheetFor` / `sheetFrame`, 6 columns, 2.25x, at most 12 sheets). The frames have the lead petal pointing up and are turned into place by `drawSheet`, so an unfurl is about 4–9 drawImage calls. Bosses go petal by petal. - Sprites are placed with `readBase`/`setFrame`/`stamp`: the transform is read once and each sprite gets its full matrix in one `setTransform`. - `pickUnfurls` gives the full unfurl to the 16 blooms closest to done (6 at quality 2); the rest crossfade over the same .55 s. - `findTremble`/`trembles` pick the one group due at `dangerY` within .8 s. - `drawFlares` adds every finale glow in one 'lighter' pass and all the glints as one star path. - `drawChainLinks` gathers up to 24 pending items that have `from` (12 at quality 1, none at quality 2) and draws them kind by kind (boss streams, sun rays, relay arcs, puff spores), then every head in one additive pass. - Every feature falls back to plain paths when there is no canvas (node), keeps save/restore balanced, and goes still under reducedMotion: a dotted thread for links, a red pip for the tremble, no flash, shards or flares. - To profile, measure on a tiny canvas with CPU throttle; headless software raster exaggerates drawImage cost (about 22 µs per call at 4x).
- Intros, harvests and boss showdowns: **The stage (app.js stage block above "Entering a current"; art.js helpers after drawCallout)** The stage gives depth levels and endless Rush a beginning, a rhythm and a villain. Everything is presentation stored on the game object, never in the save, and the engine never waits for it. Hitboxes never move: only the drawing is offset, which bloom draws from the enter fields. **app.js.** stageFrame(dt) runs once per frame after the score snap. - **New game:** it pushes the 'title' ribbon (x 210, y 210, life 1.2), sets game.stage.introAt, plays 'intro', and tumbles the first wave. - **tumble():** sorts the open buds by x and gives each enterAt / enterDrop 48 / enterDur .4, staggered by min(.035, .35/n). It queues at most 8 'plop' cues at least 40 ms apart, timed to the first touchdown. A wave event also pushes a small 'title'. - **Each frame:** it fires due plops, steps game.harvest on real dt, and lands the boss. It also keeps the boss vine's hp, damage lag (0.1 s hold, then shrinks over 0.4 s) and falling leaves, and removes enter fields once a bud has landed. - **startHarvest() on 'cleared':** turns up to 24 bloomed buds, latest first, into orbs. Each arrival calls bumpScore() and plucks. - **bossIntro():** drops the boss from 140 px with a bounce. On landing it plays 'bossLand' and jolts, then shows a name card (kind 'title', boss true) and the vine. **art.js.** - **Callouts:** drawCallout sends kind 'title' to drawTitle (cream ribbon, or a berry/gold boss card), 'trick' to drawStamp (a -6° stamp with a glyph and colour per kind from STAMPS), and 'life' to drawLifeBand. - **drawStage(ctx, state, time, options):** draws the boss vine, the harvest orbs and the score-corner glow, all under an inverse camera transform. It then draws the intro curtain (two cached strips) while introAt is under 0.6 s old and motion is on. - **Sprites:** every piece is painted once by stageSprite(key, w, h, paint) into a 3x OffscreenCanvas, kept in a 64-entry LRU. Text sprites wait for document.fonts.check, and node falls back to live paths. The orbs share one 32 px glow sprite, and the vine leaves are cached per (max, hp). - **Boss moods:** drawBossFace has them. At half health or less: narrowed eyes, heavy brows and gritted teeth. - Soundtrack and cues (`sound.js`): Audio (sound.js, qa/test-sound.cjs, qa/test-music.cjs). sound.js holds both the cue engine and the soundtrack, synthesized entirely in code with no samples. Soundtrack: - Eleven grooves are defined in SOURCE, TUNES, PULSES and KITS: home for menus and endless Rush, and one per level keyed by Depths level key. Variants inherit their parent's feel via `from`. - Each groove is 16 beats in C major. Chords are written in scale steps through chord([degree, ...extras]), so everything built from them stays diatonic. - score(name, layer) turns a groove into notes. compose() lays them out as small jobs in an OfflineAudioContext. render() builds those jobs in about 2 ms slices, renders mono at 24 kHz (falling back to 22.05k, then 44.1k), folds the 2 s tail back over the loop point, and returns an exact loop buffer. - Loops live in a 12 MB LRU cache. One render runs at a time: the bed first, then the layers in use. - The live mix goes per-layer gain, then the groove gain, then level (menus -6 dB, pause 40%), then a lowpass (900 Hz when paused, muffle, 4200 Hz open), then the music bus (0.2, ducks -4 dB on finale, superBloom and boss), then the existing master chain. Cues go through their own bus into the same master. - All layer sources share one start time, so they stay phase-locked. Changes happen on the next beat with 120 ms ramps, through automation lanes that remember every scheduled point so nothing jumps. - app.js calls BloomSound.music.frame(state) about 10 times a second (and immediately on route, pause, result or visibility changes) with route, groove, heat, threat, superBloom, boss, paused, result, over and hidden. It returns a snapshot for QA. - Heat and threat fall slowly and released layers stay two more beats, so the band builds and thins musically. - Without OfflineAudioContext the music is silently off. The Music setting is absent from the defaults: missing means on. Cues: - Every cue stays at or under 1.1 s, in C major, with soft attacks and partials between 60 Hz and 3.6 kHz. - Blooms climb the chord the music is playing by seedStep, each flower in its own timbre: gold bell, coral marimba, lilac harp, sky glass, poppy pan. - Short noise hits (crack, pop, thock, click, burst) use one seeded noise buffer through bandpass filters. Testing: - qa/test-music.cjs drives an instrumented real-time context and a recording OfflineAudioContext. It checks the groove table, key, layer rules, beat quantizing, phase lock, render order and slicing, states, ducking and muffle, the LRU cap, and frame budget. - To listen, record in Chromium by tapping AudioDestinationNode connections with a ScriptProcessor. Any new tuning should still be checked by ear on a phone.
